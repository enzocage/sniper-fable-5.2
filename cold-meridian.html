    Field manual

    Your first contract in six steps

    1. Read the briefing on the contract board. The orange and white lines tell you the order of the objectives and the optional bonuses.
    2. Open the map (Tab). Numbers are objectives, blue triangles (V1, V2) are computed vantage points that see the target, and the blue dashed line is a cover-aware route to the first one. Click anywhere to set a waypoint that shows on your compass.
    3. Walk the route, prone near the end. Standing sprinters are seen at 170 m, crouched at 100 m, prone at 45 m. Moving makes the rifle sway.
    4. Scope in (right click) and tag. Keep the crosshair on a person for a moment to tag them. The range shows at the bottom of the scope. Set the zero (Q / E) to that range.
    5. Hold your breath (Shift) and fire. Hold breath steadies the rifle and slows time. The red dot shows where the shot lands if the assist is on.
    6. Leave through the exfil marker after the last required objective. Guards who find a body or hear a shot start searching: be gone before they do.

    Open this manual any time with H (or F1).

    Reading the scope

    • Range = height of the person (1.7 m) × 1000 ÷ height in mils. The scope also prints the exact range at the bottom.
    • Drop: the rifle is zeroed with Q / E. Set it to the target range rounded to 50 m. In between, aim a little high or low (0.1 mil per 20 m of error at 400 m).
    • Wind: the arrow at the bottom shows wind direction and speed. Hold off about crosswind (m/s) × range (m) ÷ 4500 mils into the wind. 6 m/s at 600 m is about 0.8 mil.
    • Moving targets: a walking guard (1.3 m/s) at 300 m needs roughly 0.4 s of lead, about 0.3 mil.
    • Steadiness: prone with bipod is steadiest, standing and moving are the worst. Hold breath for the shot; you can only do it for a few seconds and then gasp.
    • Penetration: armour piercing goes through crates and planks (and up to three people). Standard stops at the first thing.
    • Bullet cam: a long shot on a target triggers slow motion. It is purely cosmetic: the bullet has already been calculated.

    Staying unseen

    • Exposure bar: fills when a guard sees you, when a body is found or when they hear a shot. At full, the alarm goes.
    • Vision: guards spot a standing player at about 170 m, crouched at 100 m, prone at 45 m; further in the open, less in grass and at night, fog or rain.
    • Noise: an unsuppressed shot is heard up to 520 m away (360 m in rain). A suppressor cuts that to 70 m. Thunder, church bells and ship horns cover one shot completely: you will see SOUND COVER at the top.
    • Bodies: a guard walking past a body raises the alarm. Kill someone out of sight of others, or use the lure round to pull neighbours away first.
    • Sirens: if the siren still stands when the alarm goes, reinforcements arrive and targets run for the road. Shoot the siren first if you plan a loud approach.
    • Generators: shooting one cuts the lights and cuts guard sight by 40%. Lit windows and lamps also make you easier to see from a distance.
    • Counter-snipers: a bright glint on a tower means that someone has seen you. Within 3 s you must move or shoot first.
    • Enemy tactics: once alerted, guards do not charge you. They use the radio (95 m, only 22 m once the relay is destroyed), run from cover to cover, peek out to fire, flank, and search the last place they saw you. A shot you fire is heard; they come to where they think it came from.
    • Hidden snipers: some contracts hide marksmen in camouflage, lying still in the terrain. You will not see them. A shot from one shows as a red mark on your compass for a few seconds. Scope the area to tag them. After a shot they may crawl to another position.
    • Vehicles: enemy patrol jeeps and trucks drive the roads and dismount when they notice you or when you shoot them. After a siren a truck of reinforcements drives in. Your own jeep is loud: guards within about 100 m hear it, and it is easy to see, but it is fast and can run people over (and civilians cost money).
    • Civilians cost $1,500 each. Officers that are not your target cost $1,000.

    Contracts and the map

    • White lines on the objective list are required, gold are optional with a payout. Locked objectives need an earlier one first.
    • Vantage points (V1, V2) are computed from the real terrain: they see the target from 130-450 m, are away from guard posts and are scored for height, distance from your insertion and risk.
    • Routes avoid slopes, water and the area around guards. Blue goes to the first vantage point, gold from there to the exfil.
    • Intel: red dashed shapes are where your targets move. Tagged guards show their patrol loops and sight radius (shaded disc).
    • Grid: letters across, numbers down, 100 m squares. Use grid references with the cursor read-out for range and bearing.
    • Waypoint: click the map to drop a marker. It appears on the compass and on the minimap in the corner.
    • Payout: contract fee, optional objectives and bounties, field bonuses (headshot, long range, moving target, counter-sniper, sound cover, collateral), minus penalties.

    Glossary

    Zero
    The distance at which the crosshair is exactly where the bullet lands.
    Mil
    Milliradian, 1/1000 of the distance: 1 m at 1000 m, 10 cm at 100 m.
    Vantage
    A firing position with a clear line to a target and good cover.
    Tag
    Keeping the crosshair on a person marks them on compass and map.
    Counter-sniper
    An enemy marksman who targets you if he spots you. Shooting him first pays a bonus.
    Lure
    A round that does no harm but draws guards to the impact.
    Sound cover
    A loud event (thunder, bell, horn) that hides your shot from the guards.
    Exfil
    The extraction point. It appears when all required objectives are done.
